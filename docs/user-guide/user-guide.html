1. What the module does

Amazon Marketplace Pro connects a PrestaShop store to Amazon through Amazon's official Selling Partner API, so that you can run your Amazon business from the back office you already use. It covers the whole selling cycle:

  • Listing. Your PrestaShop products become Amazon listings. The module downloads Amazon's attribute requirements for each product type and turns them into a form (a profile), so listings are built with the right shape. PrestaShop combinations become Amazon variations.
  • Price and stock. Prices with markups, rounding, sale windows and business (B2B) tiers; quantities with a safety buffer. Only what changed is sent, so large catalogues stay within Amazon's limits.
  • Orders. Amazon orders become PrestaShop orders with your carriers, statuses, invoices and taxes. Shipment confirmations and tracking go back to Amazon automatically. Orders you ship and orders Amazon ships (FBA) are told apart.
  • Overselling protection. Stock for Amazon checkouts that are not paid yet can be reserved in PrestaShop and released if the purchase never completes.
  • Money. Competitive prices and the Buy Box are tracked and can drive repricing rules; fees are recorded per order; settlement and inventory reports are collected.
  • Compliance. GPSR responsible-person details and country of origin, VAT-aware order import, VAT invoice upload, and automatic deletion of buyer data after the retention period Amazon requires.
  • Automation. Every recurring job is a scheduled task with its own interval, run either by your hosting's cron or by the IntelliPresta scheduler.

1.1 Where your data lives

The module runs entirely inside your PrestaShop installation and calls Amazon directly from your server. Product, order and buyer data is stored only in your own database.

IntelliPresta operates one small service at intellipresta.com. It does two things and nothing else:

  • It completes the "Connect to Amazon" handshake and hands the resulting token to your shop. It keeps no product, order or buyer data.
  • If you choose the IntelliPresta scheduler (section 11.1), it stores your shop's cron address and cron token so it can call your shop every five minutes. That is opt-in and removed when you switch back to your own cron.

1.2 Requirements

PrestaShop1.6 through 9. The interface adapts to the 1.6, 1.7–9 and "Basic Edition" back-office designs. Multistore is supported: each shop connects its own Amazon account (section 11.5).
Amazon accountA Professional selling plan, active, on any of the 23 supported marketplaces (Europe, North America, Brazil, the Middle East, Africa, Japan, Australia, Singapore, India).
HostingPHP with cURL. A crontab is recommended; if your hosting has none, the IntelliPresta scheduler covers it. The optional buyer-reply feature needs PHP's OpenSSL extension, which almost every host has.
CatalogueEvery product and combination needs a reference (SKU) and a barcode (EAN or UPC). The Catalogue Check tool (section 11.3) finds the ones that are missing.

2. Getting started in five steps

  1. Install the module from Modules > Module Manager, then open Configure. All of the module's screens are on that one page, grouped in a sidebar.
  2. Choose your marketplace on Settings > Connection, in the Marketplace panel, and press Save Settings. The marketplace decides the Amazon site, the currency and the listing language.
  3. Connect. Press Connect to Amazon. You are sent to Seller Central to approve the connection, then returned to the shop. There are no credentials to copy. You can revoke the authorisation from Seller Central at any time, or press Disconnect in the module.
  4. Check the connection. Press Run a test in the green banner. It reads your recent orders and says in plain words whether everything works. Section 13 explains the possible results.
  5. Turn on automation. On System > Automation, either add the one cron line shown to your hosting control panel, or register with the IntelliPresta scheduler. Then switch on the tasks you need in the table below it. Section 11 covers this in detail.

After that, the usual order of work is: check your catalogue references (11.3), create a listing profile for each product family (5.4), run the two-step sync on Catalog > Products (5.1), and let the scheduled tasks keep everything current.

Imported Order Routing

Amazon states the delivery speed it promised the buyer; map each speed onto a PrestaShop carrier. Advanced status rules give particular orders their own status, for example Prime orders in a status the warehouse cannot miss, or FBA orders straight to Shipped. Rules are evaluated top to bottom and the first match wins. Invoice by e-mail sends the PrestaShop invoice PDF, plus an optional attachment such as your terms, when an order reaches a chosen status.

4.6 Messaging

Amazon has no API for reading what a buyer writes back; replies arrive by e-mail at your seller address. Point the module at that mailbox (IMAP host, port, user, password, folder, SSL) and it files every message quoting an Amazon order into PrestaShop's Customer Service, against the right order. Use a dedicated mailbox or an app password with read access only. Fetch buyer replies now reads unread messages; the Fetch buyer messages task does it on a timer.

5. Catalog

The Products sub-tab is where products move between the two catalogues. Both directions work the same way: first a sync reads and compares, showing a table keyed by SKU; then a second step writes. Nothing is changed until the second step.

5.1 Products: PrestaShop → Amazon

  1. Sync PS to Amazon reads your PrestaShop catalogue and marks, per SKU, what differs from Amazon: description, bullet points, brand, images, EAN and categories. The comparison table appears below the steps. Each row shows the PrestaShop side, the Amazon side and a direction badge: PS only, Amazon only, conflict or in sync.
  2. Match ASINs by EAN, optional. For products Amazon already sells, finds the existing page by barcode so you add an offer to it instead of creating a duplicate listing.
  3. Push to Amazon sends what the table marks as pending. Up to 25 SKUs go one by one and are answered at once, with a status and any issues per SKU. More than 25 go as a single feed document, which Amazon processes in the background; follow it under Feed status.
What "pending" means. A row is pending when it exists in PrestaShop and is either missing on Amazon (PS only) or differs from it (conflict). The Sync settings (4.3) decide how much of each pending row is sent: full data or offer only, images or not, and whether the delta window applies.

5.2 Products: Amazon → PrestaShop

  1. Sync Amazon to PS reads your Amazon listings and marks, per SKU, what PrestaShop lacks or holds differently. The comparison appears below. Nothing is written yet.
  2. Pick what to do with the comparison:
    • A. Create the products PrestaShop does not have. Choose a category, then Create PS products from Amazon-only listings. They are created inactive, with title, description, brand, price, stock and images, for review. Prices arrive without a tax group; assign one before activating.
    • B. Update the products PrestaShop already has. Tick the fields to overwrite (title, description and brand; price; stock; bullet points as features; deactivate what Amazon no longer carries) and press Update from Amazon. This cannot be undone, so back up first. Prices arrive tax-inclusive and are converted with each product's tax rule.

5.3 Feed status and Category Mapping

Feed status. A push of more than 25 SKUs goes as one feed. Check feed status asks Amazon whether it finished and shows accepted, error and warning counts per feed. The exact JSON of every feed is kept and can be downloaded under System > Tools.

Category Mapping. The older, simpler way to tell Amazon what type a product is: one Amazon product type and optional browse node per PrestaShop category, plus optional extra attributes as JSON. Products in a mapped category push full listing data; products in an unmapped category push offer-only. Listing Profiles (5.4) do the same job with Amazon's own attribute form and take precedence; Category Mapping remains as a fallback for categories without a profile.

Amazon Product List. A read-only view of what Amazon returns for your account.

5.4 Listing Profiles

A profile pins one Amazon product type (SHIRT, FASHION_RING, and so on) and answers, once, every attribute that type requires. Amazon publishes those requirements as a schema; the module downloads it and builds a form, so you fill in real fields instead of writing JSON. Bind the profile to PrestaShop categories, and every product in them is listed with the right shape. Each category belongs to at most one profile.

Creating a profile

  1. Press New profile and give it a name, such as "Skirts - Women".
  2. Search Amazon product types (for example "shirt"), pick one, and press Load required fields. The required attributes appear as a form; optional ones are listed below with a filter. Schemas are cached for 30 days per marketplace; Refresh from Amazon fetches a fresh copy.
  3. Set the profile options: whether products have variations and on which attribute groups (Size, Color); recommended browse nodes, required for Canada, Europe and Japan; handling time; shipping template; GTIN exemption if Amazon granted one.
  4. Tick the PrestaShop categories the profile covers, add any extra attributes as raw JSON if the form cannot express them, and press Save profile.
Variations. The base product reference is the parent SKU; each combination reference is a child SKU and needs its own barcode.

5.5 Markup & Rules

Markups are fixed values ("5.99") or percentages ("10%") applied to the exported price. Rules cascade per product: category, then manufacturer, then supplier; the first source with a value wins, and the defaults apply when none matches. The same cascade sets the handling delay and the GPSR contact.

The Category Rules, Manufacturer Rules and Supplier Rules tables hold, per entity, the price markup, shipping delay, GPSR e-mail or link, and a Sync yes/no switch. The manufacturer table also holds the country of origin. Saving queues the affected products so the next delta sync resends them.

5.6 Product Rules, Queue, Orphans

Product Rules switch individual products out of the Amazon sync or give a product its own GPSR contact, which overrides the manufacturer and supplier contacts. The same and more is available on each product's Amazon tab (section 6).

Queue lists products modified in PrestaShop or touched by rule changes. With the delta window enabled, active entries are included in the next sync even if the product itself was not modified recently. Sent entries become inactive and are purged after the configured retention. The panel offers enable all, disable all, purge expired, and remove all.

Orphans lists Amazon listings that no longer map to a sellable PrestaShop product, because the product was deleted, deactivated, or its SKU changed. Run Sync Amazon to PS first so the list reflects your live inventory, then fix the products or delete the listings with the tool in 11.3.

7. Orders

7.1 Importing orders

Importing is two steps, like the catalogue, so you can look before anything is created.

  1. Fetch & Stage Amazon Orders downloads orders inside the lookback window (4.5) and stages them, with shipping costs, tax, the buyer address (see 7.2), and whether Amazon or you fulfil them. The table shows each order's items matched to PrestaShop products, and flags Prime and Amazon Business orders.
  2. Create PrestaShop Orders turns staged orders into PrestaShop orders with your carriers, statuses, invoices and taxes, following the routing rules in 4.5. Orders that lack stock are parked (7.3) if you chose to.

The scheduled tasks Import orders and Create PrestaShop orders do the same two steps unattended, every 15 minutes by default. When an order is marked Shipped in PrestaShop, and the hook in 4.3 is on, the module confirms the shipment to Amazon with the tracking number and the mapped carrier code.

7.2 Buyer addresses from an order report

Reading the buyer's name, address and phone number directly from Amazon is coming soon. For now, the way to get them is to download the order report from Seller Central and upload it to the module. Until you do, the order shows the customer as "Amazon Buyer" and the street as "Amazon Marketplace Order".

  1. Import the orders first (7.1).
  2. In Seller Central, open Orders > Order Reports > Unshipped Orders, press Request report, and Download it when it is ready. It lists the orders you have not shipped yet, which are the ones that need an address.
  3. On the module's Orders > Orders tab, choose the file in the panel Buyer addresses from an Amazon order report and press Upload report. Upload the file as downloaded.

The result says how many orders were filled in, how many already had a full address, how many PrestaShop addresses were left alone, how many orders are not imported yet, and how many were skipped because their buyer data was already deleted. The upload follows these rules:

  • Imported orders only. Lines for orders the module does not know yet are counted, not stored. Import those orders, then upload the report again.
  • Empty fields only. Nothing Amazon already sent is overwritten.
  • Your edits stay. A PrestaShop address is filled in only while it still shows the placeholder street; an address you have corrected is left as it is. The country never changes, because it decided the tax. The customer's name replaces "Amazon Buyer" only while the customer still has that name.
  • Deleted data stays deleted. Orders whose buyer data was cleared under the retention policy (section 12) are skipped.
  • The file is not kept. The activity log records only the counts.

Uploading the same report again is harmless: every order is then reported as already complete. Details filled in from a report are deleted with the rest of the buyer data after the retention period (section 12).

7.3 Pending orders and reserved stock

Pending Orders holds Amazon orders that could not become PrestaShop orders because a product lacks stock. Restock and re-run Create PrestaShop Orders, force the order with Create anyway, or remove it.

Reserved Stock (Remote Cart) lists units held for Amazon checkouts that are not payable yet. Amazon exposes a checkout in progress as a "Pending" order; with Remote Cart on (4.5), the module takes those units out of PrestaShop stock at once so no other channel can sell them, hands them over when the order becomes payable, and returns them after the grace period if it never does. The Settle remote carts task does the settling.

7.4 Returns and cancellations

Import Returns from Amazon fetches returns and cancellations; Process Pending Returns creates PrestaShop credit slips or cancels PrestaShop orders accordingly. The Return History table shows each one with its type, refund amount, PrestaShop order and credit slip. The Import returns and Process returns tasks automate both.

7.5 Buyer messaging

Enter an Amazon order ID and press Load allowed message types; Amazon limits which message types are permitted per order. Write the message and send it, or press Request a review to send Amazon's standard review request, allowed once per order, 5 to 30 days after delivery. Replies arrive by e-mail and can be filed into Customer Service with the Messaging settings (4.6).

8. Fulfilment

FBA Inventory

Sync FBA Inventory downloads the stock Amazon holds for you: fulfillable, reserved, inbound and unfulfillable per SKU. Update PS Stock from FBA writes the fulfillable quantity into PrestaShop, for merchants whose stock lives in Amazon's warehouses. The FBA inventory sync task runs the download hourly by default.

Multi-Channel Fulfilment

Enter a PrestaShop order ID and press Create MCF Order to have Amazon ship a PrestaShop order from your FBA inventory.

Multi-Account

Add further Amazon marketplaces, each with its own or the primary seller ID, and choose per marketplace whether orders, products and stock sync. The Import orders, all marketplaces and Product sync, all marketplaces tasks cover them. The primary marketplace from Settings is always active.

9. Money

Repricing

Three buttons, in order: Fetch Competitive Pricing downloads the Buy Box price, the lowest price and the number of offers for each of your listings; Apply Pricing Rules computes a suggested price per SKU; Push Suggested Prices sends the ones you approve. The Buy Box Status table shows your price against the market and whether you hold the Buy Box.

A pricing rule has a type (match or beat the lowest price, match or beat the Buy Box, or a fixed margin), an adjustment as a percentage or a fixed amount, and a minimum and maximum price as guard rails. The Repricing cycle task runs fetch and apply on a timer; pushing stays manual unless you enable it there.

Fees

Fetch Order Fees pulls referral fees, FBA fees and commissions per order from Amazon's Finances API, so the true margin of every sale is visible. The summary groups them by fee type. The Fetch fees task runs every six hours by default.

Promotions

Import the promotions applied to Amazon orders, export PrestaShop cart rules for tracking, or create PrestaShop cart rules from Amazon promotions. The history table shows each promotion with its direction.

10. Insights

Request Amazon's Merchant Listings, Settlement, or FBA Inventory reports. Amazon prepares reports in the background; Poll Pending Reports checks for finished ones and downloads them, and the history table shows status and row counts. The Poll reports task does the polling every 30 minutes by default.

11. System

11.1 Automation

A shop only runs code when a request arrives, so something has to call it on a timer. Choose who does the calling:

Nothing sits between your shop and its schedule.Your hosting has a crontab or a scheduled-task feature, which most do.

11.3 Tools

Catalogue Check. Amazon matches everything on the reference and the barcode. Products missing either, or sharing a reference with another product, cannot be listed or matched to incoming orders. Check my catalogue lists them.

Reference & Barcode Editor. Export every product and combination as CSV, fix references and barcodes in a spreadsheet, and upload the file back. Only reference, EAN, UPC and supplier reference are written. This edits your PrestaShop catalogue directly and cannot be undone; back up first, and do it before you publish, or you will create duplicate SKUs on Amazon.

Delete Listings from Amazon. Finds offers for products you no longer sell, whether deleted, disabled, or switched off for Amazon, and lets you review the list before deleting the selected ones. Deletion removes the offer and its history on Amazon, which is not reversible. To stop selling temporarily, publish quantity 0 instead (4.2).

Submitted Feed Payloads. The exact JSON sent to Amazon for each feed, downloadable by feed ID. Amazon support will ask for it when a listing is rejected for a reason the report does not explain.

11.4 Logs

The last 50 entries of the module's activity log, with date, level, source and message. Errors are highlighted. This is the first place to look when a scheduled task shows "Failed".

11.5 Several shops (multistore)

When PrestaShop runs more than one shop, the module works for each shop separately. Choose the shop in the shop list at the top of the back office, then open the module; its page shows the shop's name under the title.

WhatWith several shops
Amazon connectionEach shop connects its own Amazon seller account with Connect to Amazon. A seller account can be connected to only one shop for the same marketplace, so two shops never import the same orders.
Orders, products, stock and pricesEach shop imports its own Amazon orders and creates them in that shop, and sends only its own products, stock and prices.
AutomationEach shop has its own schedule, cron line and token (11.1). Add one cron line per shop, or register each shop with the IntelliPresta scheduler.
Profiles, category mappings, shipping templates, pricing rules and product rulesShared by all shops. A shop can save its own version, which applies to that shop only. In a shop, the ones shared by all shops are marked All shops.
Buyer data deletionRuns for every shop, each with its own retention period (section 12).
With "All shops" selected, the settings you save become the defaults for every shop. A shop that saved its own value keeps it, and the page names those shops. Connecting to Amazon, importing orders, sending products and changing the schedule work on one shop at a time, so choose a shop first. Lists show every shop, with a Shop column.

12. Buyer data and compliance

Buyer data deletion

Amazon's Data Protection Policy requires buyer data to be deleted within 30 days of delivery. With Buyer data deletion on, which is the default, the module clears the buyer's name, e-mail, street, city, region, postcode, phone and the stored Amazon payload from its own order table once an order is finished and older than the retention period you set. Order totals, tax, fees and the destination country stay, so reporting and VAT records are unaffected. The Orders settings show how many orders are waiting to be cleared and offer Clear them now; the Purge buyer data past retention task does it daily.

Turning deletion off means keeping Amazon buyer data longer than Amazon permits. Only do so under a legal obligation you can show.

Also anonymise the PrestaShop customer and address is off by default on purpose. PrestaShop renders invoices from the stored address, so anonymising it changes documents you may be legally required to keep intact, and an invoice you must keep is exactly the exception Amazon's policy allows. When on, a customer is anonymised only if every order they have is a cleared Amazon order, and city, postcode and country are kept so the VAT treatment stays justifiable.

GPSR and country of origin

The EU General Product Safety Regulation requires a responsible-person contact on listings. Set it per product (section 6), per manufacturer or supplier (5.5), and choose which of the two wins. The contact must already be registered in Seller Central under Account Health > Product Policy Compliance. Country of origin is set per manufacturer.

VAT

Imported orders can use Amazon's reported tax amounts or your own tax rules (4.5). Sellers enrolled in Amazon's VAT Calculation Service can have their PrestaShop invoices uploaded automatically.

13. Troubleshooting

Reading the test result

The result appears under the green banner in Settings > Connection. When the check fails, the technical details are also written to the Logs tab (11.4); support will ask for them.

ResultMeaning and what to do
"Everything works: the module can reach your Amazon account."The connection works. If a sync still fails, the reason is in the Logs tab.
"Amazon refused access to your seller account."The connection itself is fine, but Amazon will not serve data for this seller. Check in Seller Central that the account is active and on the Professional selling plan. If it is, press Disconnect, then Connect to Amazon again.
"Amazon no longer accepts this connection."The connection was removed in Seller Central. Press Disconnect, then Connect to Amazon again.
"Amazon or the IntelliPresta connection service could not be reached."Usually a passing network problem. Try again in a few minutes; if it keeps happening, contact support.
"This server cannot open a secure connection to Amazon."The server's security certificates are out of date. Ask your hosting provider to update them.

Common situations

SymptomCause and remedy
"No products with a reference (SKU) found" after Sync PS to AmazonProducts have no reference in the field chosen as SKU source (4.2). Fill them in, or use the Reference & Barcode Editor (11.3).
Push accepted, but the listing on Amazon is offer-onlyThe product's category has neither a profile nor a category mapping, or "Send full product data" is No (4.3).
Listing rejected with a missing-attribute errorAmazon decides required fields dynamically. Open the profile, find the attribute in the optional list, fill it in, save, and push again. The feed payload (11.3) shows exactly what was sent.
Imported orders show 0% VATChoose "Apply my PrestaShop tax rules" in 4.5.
A scheduled task shows "Failed"The message column and the Logs tab give the reason. Press Run now to retry after fixing it. After repeated failures, press Run a test (4.1).
Nothing runs although tasks are onNo cron is calling the shop. Confirm the cron line exists in your hosting panel, or that the shop is registered with the IntelliPresta scheduler and reachable over HTTPS.
Bounced e-mails to Amazon buyersAmazon relay addresses expire. Enable the anonymised e-mail (4.5).

Getting help

E-mail support@intellipresta.com. Include the module version, the PrestaShop version, the relevant lines from the Logs tab, and for listing problems the feed ID or the SKU. We answer within one business day.

14. Glossary

SKUYour own identifier for a product on Amazon. The module builds it from a PrestaShop field, usually the reference.
ASINAmazon's identifier for a catalogue page. Several sellers can offer the same ASIN.
EAN / UPC / GTINProduct barcodes. Amazon uses them to match your product to an existing page.
Listing vs offerA listing is the catalogue page with title, description and images. An offer is your price and stock on that page.
Product typeAmazon's category schema (SHIRT, FASHION_RING) that decides which attributes a listing must carry.
ProfileThe module's saved answers for one product type, bound to PrestaShop categories.
FeedA batch document sent to Amazon and processed in the background. Used for pushes of more than 25 SKUs.
MFN / AFN / FBAMerchant Fulfilled Network: you ship. Amazon Fulfilled Network, also Fulfillment by Amazon: Amazon ships from its warehouses.
MCFMulti-Channel Fulfilment: Amazon ships an order from another channel, such as your own shop, from your FBA stock.
Buy BoxThe default offer shown on an Amazon product page. Repricing aims to win or keep it.
RDTRestricted Data Token: the mechanism through which Amazon releases buyer addresses to the shop for one call at a time.
GPSRThe EU General Product Safety Regulation, which requires a responsible-person contact on listings.
VCSAmazon's VAT Calculation Service, whose enrolled sellers upload their own VAT invoices.
Delta windowThe period within which a product must have changed to be included in a push.
Remote cartAn Amazon checkout in progress, exposed as a Pending order, whose stock the module can reserve.
